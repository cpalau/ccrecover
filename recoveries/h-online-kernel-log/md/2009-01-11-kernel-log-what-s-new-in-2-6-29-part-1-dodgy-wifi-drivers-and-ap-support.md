# Kernel Log: What's new in 2.6.29 - Part 1: Dodgy Wifi drivers and AP support

**Dj Walker-Morgan**

See – [Part 2 of Whats new in 2.6.29](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-What-s-new-in-2-6-29-Part-2-WiMAX-739621.html "Kernel Log: What's new in 2.6.29 - Part 2: WiMAX – 12 January 2009, 10:19").

![](../site/imgs/43/3/6/6/0/5/1/93e8ea3d0acdf147.jpg) Scarcely two weeks after the release of [Linux 2.6.28](../site/news/item/Linux-2-6-28-Released-739471.html "Linux 2.6.28 Released – 25 December 2008, 07:26"), Linus Torvalds has integrated comprehensive changes for kernel version 2.6.29 into the main development branch. As of Friday morning, he had added a whopping 7550 patches that changed 8388 files and included more than 1,061,513 new, changed, or moved, lines of code. Over the weekend, the merge window closed and the second phase of the development cycle, which usually lasts some eight to ten weeks, has started with the release of 2.6.29-RC1. In the second phase only corrections, or small changes that do not threaten the code, will find their way into the main development branch.

A significant part of the changes integrated up to now includes a long list of improvements in the kernel's network support features. Since the network support features are the most significant additions to the kernel, a round-up of these changes kicks off this "What's new in 2.6.29" series. Should any other noteworthy patches for the network subsystem find their way into the main development branch in the coming weeks, we will sum them up in the final instalment of this series – shortly before Torvalds releases 2.6.29. On the occasion of the release, a comprehensive Kernel Log will again sum up the most important changes reported in the course of the "What's new in 2.6.29" series.

### Dodgy network drivers

Following the [integration](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-What-s-coming-in-2-6-28-Part-3-Dodgy-drivers-737747.html "Kernel Log: What's coming in 2.6.28 - Part 3: Dodgy drivers – 22 October 2008, 09:29") in 2.6.28 of Greg Kroah-Hartman's [staging kernel branch](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-Linux-staging-branch-will-help-with-integration-Linux-kernel-2-6-25-6-and-2-6-26-rc6-735507.html "Kernel Log: Linux-staging branch will help with integration; Linux kernel 2.6.25.6 and 2.6.26-rc6 – 17 June 2008, 11:31") into the Linux main development branch, the self-styled "[maintainers of crap](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/736852)" have [added numerous additional drivers](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/776353), in the kernel's staging area, that do not meet the kernel developers' quality standards. Among the offenders were the [rt2860](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=91980990527258a075361490cecadbb7356fc0d2) and [rt2870](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=c55519ff75224222f4668c92ae3733059269f575) Wifi drivers for the Ralink Wifi chips found in some of the new netbooks and low-end notebooks. Other new entries in the staging area are the [otus](http://git.kernel.org/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=4bd43f507c7e2f225f58235226a8381fd6bbff1a) driver, [released in October](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-New-Atheros-WLAN-drivers-and-stable-kernels-radeon-vs-radeonhd-737773.html "Kernel Log: New Atheros WLAN drivers and stable kernels, radeon vs. radeonhd – 24 October 2008, 10:59"), for the Atheros UB81, UB82 and UB83 WLAN chips, as well as the [agnx](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=0f22aab89715916c100b5fa7d146c497d99d45ca) and [rtl8187se](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=c8d86be38785705aca77e33933298c320a1bf2a5) drivers for the Airgo AGNX00 WLAN chip and the Realtek RTL8187SE WLAN chip. There was also a comprehensive scrub and restructuring done on the code for the wlan-ng framework included in 2.6.28. Developers [swapped](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=02227c28391b5059a7710d6039c52912b0ee2c1d) the at76_usb staging driver, also included in 2.6.28, for a variant based on the mac80211 Linux WLAN stack – actually, the author of the driver had [another solution in mind](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel.wireless.general/26045/focus%3D26487), so it would be no surprise if additional changes are made to the patch, or if it is withdrawn entirely.

Also, the [benet](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=479e2f445faf7a04d75f807b81b316ebc5b043ca) network driver for ServerEngines' BladeEngine (EC 3210) 10Gb network adapter is a new addition to the staging area. Whether users of the mainstream distributions and their kernels will see tangible advantages from the inclusion of all these staging drivers depends on which distribution they are using. Administrators of the distributions' kernels activate only some of the staging drivers, or only partially activate them, since they do not meet the normal quality standards of the kernel developers. The drivers' failure to meet these standards is also the reason the kernel is marked with a "TAINT_CRAP" flag when it is loading them. This makes it clear in users' error reports that a "crappy" driver has "besmirched" the kernel and may have been responsible, or partly responsible, for problems. However, in the absence of other drivers, users who simply want to use their hardware may not give a hoot about the driver, as long as it does not cause any serious problems. Network manager/developer Dan Williams made it known in a recent Fedora list post that he does not think much of the staging drivers ([1](https://www.redhat.com/archives/fedora-devel-list/2009-January/msg00474.html), [2](https://www.redhat.com/archives/fedora-devel-list/2009-January/msg00473.html)). He said that he would ignore bugs involving staging drivers, *"Basically, I'm going to ignore any issues that come in from these drivers because they aren't accepted upstream wireless drivers, despite what gregkh (who's not a wireless developer) tries to make them."*

**More than a thousand other changes**

Network subsystem administrator David S. Miller did not leave it to Greg Kroah-Hartman alone to submit all of the network updates; he collected more than a thousand network-related patches himself and sent them to Torvalds. ([1](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.network/115244), [2](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.network/115597), [3](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.network/116076)).

**New and removed WLAN drivers, AP mode**

Support for operation as an access point (AP), which has been in the kernel's Wifi stack for some time, albeit deactivated, has now [been activated](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=fbf189273926d83d71c4c321f1215162569506ac) ([documentation](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=f9f88fed3433139b58962011c81597b44fd48458), [support in nl80211](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=90c97a040d6b08cc4890328aa262fdc37336ab01)). However, the kernel does not handle the actual AP administration functions itself, but rather leaves them to the current versions of [hostapd](http://hostap.epitest.fi/hostapd/). The WLAN drivers have to support AP mode as well, although this is [not the case](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=33fd8195529d74c0fe23cddd1c76fe7e03bbd324) with the kernel's drivers for the Intel WLAN modules found in Centrino notebooks and others. Developers are expanding the ath5k and p54 WLAN drivers, to support AP mode ([1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=da966bcae70e4012b7d999820b728dd6502047e0), [2](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=e5ea92a7528d304e8e327d0d261653e98b163e8a)).

The kernel hackers have extended the Ath9k WLAN driver, which has been AP-compatible for some time, to make it support the AR9285 Atheros WLAN chip ([1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=e8fbc99edfe0efa0b42f04587a79a6b3371f961a), [2](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=e7594072a5b918510c937c1ab0acad4e8a931bc7)). Developers have comprehensively revised the rate control API and HT handling for the mac80211 WLAN stack ([1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=e6a9854b05c1a6af1308fe2b8c68f35abf28a3ee), [2](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=ae5eb02641233a4e9d1b92d22090f1b1afa14466), [3](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=d9fe60dea7779d412b34679f1177c5ca1940ea8d)). Also, the kernel hackers have added [802.11d support](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=3f2355cb9111ac04e7ae06a4d7044da2ae813863) to cfg80211 and mac80211. Parts of the outdated Linux WLAN stack, still needed for the ipw2*x*00 and hostap drivers, have been [shuffled around](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=274bfb8dc5ffa16cb073801bebe76ab7f4e2e73d) and [hidden](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=4acf07497140b1504a799d6a1b4da7d1b1e35af5), which can cause problems when compiling some WLAN drivers that are dependent on the official Linux kernel.

**LAN related changes**

The [Generic Receive Offload (GRO) Infrastructure](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=d565b0a1a9b6ee7dff46e1f68b26b526ac11ae50) has been added for LAN drivers. Other new additions include the [smsc911x](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=fd9abb3d97c2ab883e4732ec1214fe64190236e7) driver for SMSC 's LAN911x and LAN921x LAN chips, as well as the [smsc9420](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=2cb377283f3469d66f0ea7358015abfe8366e5d0) driver for the 100 Mb LAN9420 chip also made by SMSC. The bnx2 driver [is now compatible](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=1caacecb7cb2b72e798f06a32b5061075cf397fa) with the BCM5716S Broadcom chip and the Broadcom PHY module now works with the [BCM57780](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=2fbb69aa576f4392c4a04e6874fa429b8bde0f68) and [BCM50610](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=772638b6c87da7043c50914dbb033c08155508dd) chips. There are new [improvements for the 5785](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=fcb389dfd842be54545cb436b3437f07da10115c) chip in the tg3 driver for Marvell LAN chips, as well as support for the [5761S](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=c88e668b52c8cd8bc55b6734c7dfbb0cb005d445) and [57780](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=321d32a052d6b5f71111ebad4fbebea5577f8974) chips.

Following the network subsystem and subsystem drivers administrators' initially cool reception of the split in 2.6.27 between drivers and firmware, the kernel developers have now implemented the separation in numerous network drivers -- in 2.6.29 the split has been implemented in the [acenic](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=949b42544a20fb22800e244a004ff45bd359a21b), [e100](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=9ac32e1bc0518b01b47dd34a733dce8634a38ed3), [starfire](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=cfc3a44c3c32abe48898398d9a92e8524c976803) and [tg3](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=077f849de42e58172e25ccb24df4c1a13e82420c) drivers. Developers have [removed](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=6b1abbaefa31b84cc02bf4006ba8a63393de1136) the eepro100 driver for Intel 100 Mb LAN chips, which was replaced long ago by the e100 driver. The [hci_usb](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=12421b40b81d101d7535e03f1af197365adc932b) Bluetooth driver also had to go, since it was made obsolete by the btusb driver some time ago.
