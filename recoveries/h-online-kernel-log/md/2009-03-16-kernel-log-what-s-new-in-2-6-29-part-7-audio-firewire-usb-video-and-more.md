# Kernel Log: What's new in 2.6.29 - Part 7: Audio, FireWire, USB, Video and more

**Dj Walker-Morgan**

On Thursday night, Linus Torvalds [released an eighth pre-release version of Linux 2.6.29](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/806262) and hinted that this could be the final 2.6.29 release candidate, with the main development tree now stabilising. He did not, however, completely rule out further pre-release versions, "*\[...\]it seems to be stabilising to the point where I'm hoping that we're approaching a final 2.6.29, and this might be the last -rc. We'll have to see.*"). Kernel Log is taking this opportunity to continue the "What's coming in 2.6.29" series with an overview of driver news from a range of areas.

### Audio and video

This development cycle once again saw hundreds of major and minor changes to the [Alsa](http://www.alsa-project.org/) framework and drivers (including [1](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/772423), [2](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/776153), [3](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/779333), [4](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/781341), [5](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/794194)). According to [information in the kernel](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=a7d26f84cb76b962217ce277c3e3a9748a26ac11), audio support in Linux 2.6.29 is now at the level of Alsa 1.0.18a, but it will in fact include many of the changes in the current 1.0.19 Alsa driver version. These include, for example, the driver for HD audio codecs [being split](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=1289e9e8b42f973f2ab39e5f4f2239ff826c27e9) into multiple files. As a consequence, in future, when compiling the audio drivers as a module, different, generally vendor-specific modules, will be responsible for HD audio codecs. However, these will no longer need to be loaded manually â€“ rather the driver for the HD audio device will automatically detect which codec is connected and request the respective module.

The developers have also added support for audio output via HDMI with Intel and Nvidia chipsets to the HD audio driver (e.g. [1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=91504877c50a792412e2043a1c2099f054d7254a)). The energy saving features for [AC97](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=36adba1fc79851949c4792f2e9b4d0dddbc6d5e4) and [HD audio](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=02834f112d159eb87b584c7df56bddc5beeff5b3) chips are no longer considered experimental â€“ users wanting to extend their laptop's battery life should, unless the kernel of the Linux distribution they are using has already done so, activate this feature during kernel configuration or [later](http://www.lesswatts.org/tips/misc.php). The pcxhr driver for Digigram's eponymous audio chips has been substantially enhanced ([1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=9d948d270010e3552c94281bab75694580ca23e9), [2](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=c0193f39f43c79bde6c1c5804f5315f3983152b5)). Many new drivers have also been added to the ASoC (ALSA System on Chip) framework.

As with pretty much every new kernel version, the developers have significantly extended the whitelists for automatic use of hardware specific "quirks", including in this case for the [Dell Inspiron Mini9](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=57d139278e6c246d78f71e4bf0e0d15bb0390646), [Fujitsu-Siemens Amilo Xa3530](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=ef8ef5fb1027b56f867d4b913cf52bfdc610d2a7) and [HP 6730B](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=11d518e07d700eeb5bcec36bfd5f501e405230dd) netbooks and laptops.

### V4L/DVB

It's not just the audio subsystem that's been souped up â€“, the video4Linux/DVB subsystem has also been extended or modified by hundreds of patches ([1](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/773614), [2](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/774762), [3](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.comp.video.video4linux/42085), [4](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/788399), [5](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.drivers.video-input-infrastructure/1746), [6](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.drivers.video-input-infrastructure/2242)). Two new Gspca drivers have been added â€“ [ov534](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=fbb4c6d20f29f2b10daad31cc6238d91f93d70d4) addresses the Sony Playstation EYE, whilst [stv06xx](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=4c98834addfee3fdd42c505c37569261bf669d94) is tailored to various older Logitech Quickcam cameras. Until now, these cameras have usually required one of two drivers going by the name qc-usb, which have never made the leap into the Linux main development tree. Also new are the [cx24113](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=4c48ae8e8a490f16abbd874602135bcd83c84f26) driver for the CX24113 chip used in the Technisat Skystar2 revision 2.8 and the [omap2cam](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=39aee69a166b775a38ed0053596cdb8e717ae315) driver for the camera on the Nokia N800/N810 Internet Tablet. Many more new drivers can be located via the links below.

### FireWire and USB

Stefan Richter has introduced numerous enhancements and extensions into the FireWire subsystem, which he maintains (e.g. [1](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel/777023), [2](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.kernel.firewire.devel/13042)). These provide enhancements for asynchronous data transfer and [remove](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=82d4b90debaa7ab3590335c1b641eb3d2ebb164e) the 800Â Mb/s maximum speed limit from the old FireWire stack. Very late in the second half of the development cycle, the [firedtv](http://www.firedtv.bbackx.com/) (formerly firesat) V4L/DVB driver has found its way into the main development tree [via the FireWire development tree](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.drivers.video-input-infrastructure/2130). It supports the [FireDTV and FloppyDTV](http://www.digital-everywhere.com/en/alcms/index.php?start) FireWire TV hardware developed by Digital Everywhere and marketed by Elgato.

Both [Wimax code](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-What-s-new-in-2-6-29-Part-2-WiMAX-739621.html "Kernel Log: What's new in 2.6.29 - Part 2: WiMAX â 12 January 2009, 10:19") and extended support for USB OTG (On-The-Go) have made their way into the main development tree via the USB development tree ([1](http://git.kernel.org/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commitdiff;h=b8da8677d4f88db066c1cfe34529d970a060de46), [2](http://git.kernel.org/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=6084f1bf0c51a99cbba612ee90a4607cffb8b042), [3](http://git.kernel.org/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commitdiff;h=3cb22d658668234edbe6dcb165501e9ef0c0a059), [4](http://git.kernel.org/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commitdiff;h=9ebd9616648bc0e47e7f8e1898c919305f1e6347)). Other changes should accelerate data transfer via Wireless USB.

### Staging driver developments

The [first part](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-What-s-new-in-2-6-29-Part-1-Dodgy-Wifi-drivers-and-AP-support-739619.html "Kernel Log: What's new in 2.6.29 - Part 1: Dodgy Wifi drivers and AP support â 11 January 2009, 16:08") of the "What's coming in 2.6.29" series mentioned a number of network and WLAN drivers which the kernel development team had included in the kernel's [staging directory](../site/news/item/Kernel-Log-What-s-coming-in-2-6-28-Part-3-Dodgy-drivers-737747.html "Kernel Log: What's coming in 2.6.28 - Part 3: Dodgy drivers â 22 October 2008, 09:29"). There have also been plenty of other new inclusions in the staging directory. The [commit](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=3fedd14818592016f7ffd84dfe134881b3896ecf) which adds [Meilhaus](http://www.meilhaus.de/en/home/)' ME-IDS drivers totals around 1.2Â Mb. The kernel development team have also included the [Comedi framework](http://www.comedi.org/) and numerous kernel extensions from Google's Android in the staging directory. Details of these and other staging drivers incorporated into fpr 2.6.29 can be found in the links at the end of this article.

### Even more drivers

The ps3vram driver ([1](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=cffb4add03b1fc83026b06dc3664279cfbf70155), [2](http://git.kernel.org/git/?p=linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux-2.6.git;a=commit;h=0a2d15b928e0b1673d4ed5f48d95af211b6fcc06)) allows the PS3's video RAM to be used for swapping or temporary file storage. The kernel development team is, however, planning to remove this driver shortly before 2.6.29 is released and replace it with another ([1](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.ports.ppc.embedded/21609/), [2](http://thread.gmane.org/gmane.linux.ports.ppc.embedded/21854/focus%3D21939)).
